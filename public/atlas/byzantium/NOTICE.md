# Detailed Byzantine source layer

Source: user-supplied archive of Lucius, *Historical maps of the Byzantine Empire 330–1453*, https://lucius-note.net/byzantium/. This atlas uses 39 supplied frames from 395 through 1453. Source filenames and SHA-256 are pinned in `manifest.json`; source URLs may later be revised by their author. ©Lucius, All Rights Reserved. No CC, GPL or other open license is asserted for these uploaded images or their adaptations. This collection is separate from the GPL Historical Basemaps overview.

Six conic models were fitted to printed graticules; this is a numerical registration model, not a claim about the author's undisclosed original CRS. All fitted parameters and control-point records are retained in the manifest. Five groups use held-out grid intersections; the early 395 fit's residual is an internal consistency check using radial samples of the same globally fitted meridians. Other frames are registered by blue coast/river keypoints with a similarity transform. Held-out registration RMS covers consensus points; rejected/outlier counts remain visible and are not silently treated as accurate matches.

The color-classification copy suppresses typography/grey graticules by 3-pixel inpainting, preserves blue coastlines/rivers, and closes 3×3-pixel gaps. Colored areas are traced as polygons, including nested rings; invalid rings are repaired while retaining polygonal parts. Contours use 1.25-source-pixel simplification, five decimal coordinate rounding, a 12-pixel² minimum island area and a 240-pixel² minimum water-hole area and a 5000-pixel² minimum non-water-hole area (to avoid tracing white text halos as lakes). These operations can shift borders under labels, omit small islands, or alter narrow cut-outs. They do not establish historically surveyed borders. Compare the original for critical work.

The source annotations are independently remapped to Web Mercator as local 3200-pixel-wide WebP images. Typography is **not** suppressed in these displayed images. Source frames and their legends/copyrights are available from the author's original PNG links. Outer frame and legend swatches are excluded from the geographic raster footprint, so a legend color rectangle cannot become a false country. When enabled, the full source raster covers the overview within its footprint; administrative lines and names are imagery, not individually selectable polygons.

No temporal interpolation. Byzantine display uses the last source frame at or before the selected event year; this is not evidence that no changes occurred between snapshots. 1204 and 1261 are explicitly marked as transitional years without a matching source frame. The 1453 source shows possessions **before the capture of Constantinople on 29 May**. Nicaea, Epirus/Thessalonica, Trebizond and Theodoro retain separate labels/colors where the supplied source does so.

Other countries retain unchanged overview geometries for 500–1400; they are unconfigured outside that range. Neutral land is not a historical claim of an empty or stateless region. Underlying land/rivers use modern Natural Earth references.

Reproduce from the supplied PNGs with `scripts/build-byzantine-atlas.py` and the committed control-point configuration. Data preparation is offline and is not part of the application build. `frames.json` stores vector geometry and raster metadata. `manifest.json` stores source/destination hashes, transform matrices, reference controls and processing thresholds.
